## Decision

No identified, measured change plausibly reaches 30 fps on the sampled P7 workload by itself. The latest paired aura-owner measurement is 2,197,886 guest cycles per advancing full loop, including update/audio/render/presentation, against roughly 559,000 cycles for a 30 fps period: about 1.64 million cycles, or 74.6%, must disappear. This is a replacement-loop emulator measurement, not a hardware frame rate; presentation waits and code placement complicate extrapolation. Earlier diagnostic scopes were recorded on earlier source and overlap, so their totals cannot be added or subtracted from this latest baseline as a forecast.

Rank projects as follows; numbers are scope ceilings or historical signals, not promised savings:

1. **Re-profile and optimize exact order setup / detection on the current source.** The older P7 diagnostic attributed 633,359 cycles/update to due Arbiter cache processing within 759,493 setup cycles; the later detector-list and optical-snapshot optimizations already materially changed this work, and the owner-bucket change saved only 4,700 cycles (0.213%) on its paired normal-vector run. Instrument the current cache refresh by valid/due rows, detector list construction, powered cannons, geometry, source-owner mapping, and empty vs nonempty passes. Pursue only an evidenced repeated computation whose result can be reused within the existing source tick, preserving raw-owner distinctions, exact ordering, clock/reset behavior and fallback. The old 633k is a stale inclusive upper bound, not current attainable savings. Compare full per-update Unit/Building/cache/due streams to the baseline and independent all-pairs geometry and malformed-owner oracle across deaths, transport, slot reuse, owner changes, cache resets and boundary positions; use paired normal-vector P7 tape and other race/showcase windows, verify stack, BSS/IWRAM and all production profiles. Reject if net normal-vector gains do not justify complexity.

2. **Attribute and reduce synchronous movement/route work without changing answers or timing.** Earlier P7 measured 409,052 mean movement cycles and a 2,699,554-cycle movement maximum at call 8347; these are instrumented pre-integration figures, not current bounds. A different historical Zerg tape found a 1,015,282-cycle `path_next_tile` inside a 1,085,003-cycle movement event. Profile current P7 call 8347 and representative blocked, dynamic, and multi-unit workloads: distinguish flood expansions, direct tests, contact masks, cache hits/rebuilds and repeated same-world queries. Exact results must preserve neighbor/tie ordering, blocked goals, dynamic occupancy and invalidation; start with intra-update reuse or an equivalent data/algorithm representation only after identifying a repeated workload. Historical route flags achieved at most 0.407% full-loop reduction in their sampled combination, and partial flood worsened peak orders, so do not simply enable previously tested flags. Differentially compare every route result and full simulation state per update with independent scalar BFS/steering oracle, including terrain changes, slot reuse, blocked/unreachable destinations and save/resume; pair mean and tail cycles on multiple current tapes, keeping product memory/stack guards. Do not stagger or defer movement.

3. **Fuse fixed-selector mission census predicates and test safe cache-validation ordering.** The current-stage diagnostic measured 87,690 cycles/update for the entire P7 trigger census and 60,832 for the nested Arbiter cache-row validation, in a separately instrumented P7 run; each is an inclusive gross ceiling, not a net saving. The census did four fixed-selector owner matches per live row. An exact candidate could validate owner once then derive the four results; separately, a VALID-bit check could avoid a full-byte zero-row check only where validity implies nonzero. Preserve invalid-owner rejection, raw identity, count/order and fallback, and do not combine the maxima as guaranteed gain. Differentially exhaust owner/active-slot/selector combinations, malformed cache words, dead/reused/transported rows and lifecycle changes against the original functions; verify per-update trigger and full persistent state plus save/load and normal-vector paired cycles. This is feasible but cannot close the 30 fps gap alone, even if both old scopes were completely eliminated.

4. **Measure rendering and presentation separately before selecting a rendering project.** The older P7 stage diagnostic measured 366,859 map and 124,900 HUD cycles, nested within the full loop; its placement and source predate current changes. A separate Zerg diagnostic found 122.64k background and 110.03k unit-draw cycles/call, with nested tracer work; `gfx_end` presentation wait is outside map/HUD and inside the loop. Measure current production-vector useful drawing, dirty work, clipping, HUD rebuilds and wait separately on busy and sparse scenes; pursue only exact caching, clipping, or lookup work with authenticated invalidation. Full-frame pixel streams for fog, overlays, HUD, scrolling, palettes, entity reuse and screen transitions plus simulation-state comparisons, varied tapes and normal-vector guards are required. The historical map scope is not a present-day savings estimate and a presentation wait is not necessarily removable compute.

## Cadence fidelity and blind spots

The owned N64 reference SHA-256 is `e7d21880c13953b45b8f8db6b2b6cfa02daa29d35b23799a967e96f21001b020`. In the interpreter observation, four watched records each had one timer decrement in every one of 126 complete global-countdown decrement intervals; each also had 15–16 reload-to-eight stores and separate zero resets. This supports investigating per-unit dispatch, not a uniform nine-update skip, one global simulation tick per countdown, or skipping an actor update. Four early-return order IDs and 36 numeric pre-handler IDs precede the later gate; movement, cooldowns, pre-handlers and exceptional resets require independent treatment. Bookends do not rule out all intervening identity changes, DMA, hidden units or other orders; normal-JIT/pause/speed and global active-update timing are not fully established. Prior installed-ares observations are documentary, not comprehensive order-cadence validation.

If cadence becomes a project, first obtain authorized, bounded reference observations of multiple order classes, movement, weapon cooldown, pre-handlers and reset/death/transport cases at several speeds and pause transitions, in the same process with independently verified identities. Relate countdown invocations to active world updates and observable outcomes before modeling individual dispatch; require per-order differential timing and full-state/gameplay proof. Do not assume that slowing any current logic is a fidelity fix or that such an investigation will save a measurable amount.

The key performance blind spots are current post-merge stage attribution; inclusive/nested timer overhead; replacement-main layout and VBlank/presentation wait; one P7 nonterminal tape rather than full missions, maps and races; frame-time tail distribution; and physical GBA results. The 158.7M-cycle Z7 maximum was a PLAY-to-RESULT/save transition and the 119.0M-cycle initial HOME load is a separate pause, not evidence of a combat/pathfinding peak. A zero-defense shortcut is rejected: current diagnostic measured a 6,269-cycle entire zero-defense scope in one 1-AI window and no zero-defense updates in sampled P7 or 3-AI windows.
